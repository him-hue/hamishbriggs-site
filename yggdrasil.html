Yggdrasil /ˈɪɡdrəsɪl/ n.

The world tree of Norse mythology: one root that binds many worlds into a single living system.

The way I see it:

AI is a computer. Computers are good at math and logic.

Creativity is human. Humans are good at imagination.

Today we use AI to be creative at the tips of the branches, and ask humans to be logical at the root.

This is backwards.

We need to

give AI charge of the syntax
so humans can own the semantics.

The problem

Growth erodes coherence.

The bigger a vision grows, the faster it loses the plot.

Every project is a nest of systems: a sentence serves a page, a page serves a strategy, a strategy serves the vision at the top. As a vision succeeds it splinters into branches, and every branch added is one more thing to hold in alignment. Quietly, out at the edges, the vision blurs. And lately, AI working from thin context at the tips only blurs it faster.

The shift

Put AI at the root, not the tips.

AI isn't the enemy; where we put it is. At the tips of the branches it guesses, and delivers something brilliant but often unmoored. Move it to the core (the root, where the vision, voice, and decisions are kept true), and it stops guessing. The human stays the author. The machine keeps everything downstream honest.

Curative AI upstream sharpens generative AI downstream.

Keep creativity human.
